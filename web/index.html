01

Spatial Scale Harmonization (~10.0 km Block Aperture)

Terrain slope, root-mean-square roughness, and local relief exhibit fractal scale dependency. Processing Earth, Mars, and Moon digital elevation models at distinct native grid resolutions introduces profound sensor artifact bias. The pipeline resamples NOAA ETOPO 2022 (Earth), MGS MOLA MEGDR (Mars), and LRO LOLA GDR (Moon) into consistent ~10 km analysis blocks to isolate true planetary geomorphic structure.

02

Multivariate Z-Score Normalization & Covariance Decoupling

Features are standardized against the continental arid Earth background distribution: \(z = (x - \mu)/\sigma\). Topographic roughness and local relief exhibit an empirical Pearson correlation of \(r = 0.987\). Standard Euclidean distance double-counts relief amplitude; Mahalanobis distance uses the inverse covariance matrix \(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}\) to project terrain features into orthogonal eigenspace.

03

Dual Engine: Directional Cosine vs. Covariance-Weighted Metric

The client application dynamically executes weighted Cosine Similarity (\(S_c\)) for angular alignment and Mahalanobis Distance (\(D_M\)) for multivariate cluster proximity, re-evaluating top candidates across the global sample pool in sub-millisecond client-side execution.

04

Scientific Limitations & Terrestrial Boundary Conditions

Planetary surface weathering operates under severe atmospheric pressure differentials: vacuum (Moon), 6 mbar \(CO_2\) (Mars), and 1,013 mbar \(N_2/O_2\) (Earth). Thermal diurnal amplitudes cannot be directly mapped without climate distortion. The system restricts Earth sampling to arid climates (\(P_{\text{annual}} < 500\text{ mm}\)) to preserve mechanical weathering and regolith fidelity.
