01

Multi-Sensor Planetary Footprints & Scale Harmonization

Terrain slope, root-mean-square roughness, and local relief exhibit fractal scale dependency. Processing Earth, Mars, and Moon digital elevation models requires analyzing them across target-appropriate geomorphic footprints (Moon ~20 km window, Mars ~220 km box, Earth ~55–111 km footprint). Because relief and roughness depend directly on window size, rankings reflect relative geomorphic comparison across available orbital datasets, not absolute matches.

02

Multivariate Z-Score Normalization & Covariance Decoupling

Features are standardized against the continental arid Earth background distribution: \(z = (x - \mu)/\sigma\). Topographic roughness and local relief exhibit an empirical Pearson correlation of \(r = 0.987\). Standard Euclidean distance double-counts relief amplitude; Mahalanobis distance \(D_M = \sqrt{(\mathbf{z}_a - \mathbf{z}_t)^T \mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{z}_a - \mathbf{z}_t)}\) uses the inverse covariance matrix \(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}\) to project terrain features into orthogonal eigenspace.

03

Dual Engine: Directional Cosine vs. Covariance-Weighted Metric

The client application dynamically executes weighted Cosine Similarity (\(S_c\)) for angular alignment and Mahalanobis Distance (\(D_M\)) for multivariate cluster proximity, re-evaluating 2,500 pre-filtered candidates in <2 ms in JavaScript while the full 500,073-cell global ranking is computed offline.

04

Terrain-First Screen & Scientific Limitations

Results represent a terrain-first screen (slope, roughness, relief + macroclimate aridity filter), not a full geologic or mineralogical match. Hyperspectral mineralogy and thermal dynamics are key future extensions. Planetary surface weathering operates under severe atmospheric pressure differentials: vacuum (Moon), 6 mbar \(CO_2\) (Mars), and 1,013 mbar \(N_2/O_2\) (Earth).

05

Shackleton Crater Resolution & Illumination Constraints

Shackleton Crater Rim (~21 km diameter) is analyzed across a ~20 km polar stereographic window (subsampled from 20 m to 100 m/pixel), yielding a compact elevation fingerprint. Furthermore, permanent shadow regions (PSRs) and volatile cold traps (~40 K) are dictated by polar low-elevation solar angles and cannot be captured by terrain morphometry alone.
